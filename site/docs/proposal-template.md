# Proposal template

**How to use this:** fill in everything in `[square brackets]`, delete the italic coaching notes, export to PDF, send within 3 days of the call.

**The one rule:** their problem comes first, in their own words. Deliverables come last. Most agencies open with a list of what they'll build — that's talking about yourself. Open with the thing that's costing them money, said back to them the way they said it. If they read the first paragraph and think *"yes, that's exactly it"*, the rest is a formality.

## 1. The problem, as you described it

> *[Quote them almost word for word from the discovery call. Use their phrasing, including the slang. "I does be answering the same three questions from morning till night and I still missing orders."]*

Here's what we heard underneath that:

- **[Failure state 1]** — *e.g. Messages come in between 9pm and 7am and nobody sees them until morning.*
- **[Failure state 2]** — *e.g. Every price question needs a person to check the stock before answering.*
- **[Failure state 3]** — *e.g. There's no way for a customer to buy anything without you typing.*

*Coaching note: three is the number. One looks thin, five looks like you're padding. If they only gave you one real problem, the call wasn't long enough — go back.*

## 2. What changes

*Coaching note: outcomes, not features. No product names in this section. They should be able to read this section alone and know what their week looks like afterwards.*

When this is in place:

- [Outcome 1 — *e.g. Anybody who messages after hours gets a real answer with a real price, in seconds, without waking you.*]
- [Outcome 2 — *e.g. The questions you answer forty times a day get answered without you.*]
- [Outcome 3 — *e.g. An order can be placed and paid for while your phone is face-down on the table.*]

**What we are not promising:** we can't guarantee where you land on Google, and we can't promise a sales figure. Anybody who does is guessing. What we promise is the system, the timeline and the response time.

*Coaching note: leave that paragraph in. Every time. It's the most trust-building thing in the document, and it costs nothing because those promises were never yours to make.*
