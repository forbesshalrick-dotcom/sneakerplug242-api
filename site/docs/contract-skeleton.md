> # ⚠️ NOT A CONTRACT YET — A BAHAMIAN ATTORNEY MUST REVIEW THIS BEFORE YOU USE IT
>
> This is a **skeleton**: a checklist of the clauses a build agreement needs and plain-English notes on what each one is for. It was **not** written by a lawyer, it has **not** been checked against Bahamian contract, consumer or data law, and it is **not** legal advice.
>
> Take it to an attorney in Nassau, have them turn it into a real agreement, and use *that*. A single review of a template you'll reuse on every client is one of the cheapest things you will ever buy — and using an unreviewed contract is exactly the kind of thing that costs a small business its first serious dispute.
>
> **Until an attorney signs off: do not send this to a client.**

# Website & systems build agreement — clause skeleton

**Between:** [Your registered business name], of [address], Nassau, Bahamas ("we", "us")
**And:** [Client legal business name], of [address] ("you", "the client")
**Dated:** [date]

---

## 1. What we're building

- Attach the signed proposal as **Schedule A** and state that it forms part of the agreement.
- Say plainly: the deliverables in Schedule A are the whole scope. Anything not listed is not included.
- Name the tier and the version of the proposal being attached (dated, so there's no argument about which draft).

*Why: nearly every dispute in this trade is a scope dispute. A dated attachment ends most of them in one sentence.*

---

## 2. Price and payment

- Total fixed price, stated in **Bahamian dollars**.
- **50% deposit** before work begins. Work does not start until it clears.
- **50% due on launch**, defined as the day the site is live on the client's domain.
- Payment window — [x] days from invoice.
- What happens if payment is late: work pauses, and the launch date moves by the same number of days.
- Whether VAT applies — **ask the attorney and your accountant**; do not guess.
- State clearly that the deposit is **non-refundable once work has begun**, and why (the slot was booked and other work turned away).

---

## 3. Third-party costs are the client's

- Domain registration and renewal
- Hosting beyond what's included in the care plan
- Payment processor fees
- Paid software, plugins, stock photography or fonts
- **AI running costs** — per-conversation charges on bot builds

State whether these are billed to the client's own accounts (preferred) or paid by us and recharged **at cost, with no markup**.

---

## 4. What the client has to do

The most common cause of a missed deadline is not the builder. Spell it out:

- Supply content, photos, prices and logins by the dates in the proposal
- Name **one person** with authority to approve work — one, not a committee
- Respond to approval requests within [x] business days
- Confirm that everything supplied is theirs to use (see §8)

**Consequence clause:** if these slip by more than [x] days, the timeline moves accordingly and we may re-book the slot. Without this clause, their delay becomes your problem.

---

## 5. Revisions

- **Two rounds** of revisions included, at the review points named in the proposal.
- Define a "round": one consolidated list of changes, not a trickle of messages over three weeks.
- Anything beyond two rounds is billed at B$[rate]/hour, quoted and approved in writing first.
- Define what counts as a **change of scope** rather than a revision — new pages, new features, a new design direction after approval.

---

## 6. Domain and hosting ownership

> **This clause is a selling point. Make it generous and put it in plain words.**

- The domain is registered **in the client's name, on the client's email account**.
- The client receives all logins and credentials at handover, and may request them at any time before that.
- We never hold a domain, hosting account or credential as leverage in a payment dispute.
- On termination for any reason, we assist with transfer at no charge.

---

## 7. Who owns the work

- On **final payment**, the client owns the finished website: the design, the content and the front-end code as delivered.
- **We retain** our underlying tools, frameworks, bot engine and anything pre-existing that we brought to the job. The client gets a licence to use those for as long as they hold an active care plan.
- We may show the finished work in our portfolio and case studies unless the client says otherwise in writing.

*Ask the attorney specifically about this split — it's the clause most likely to be drafted badly, and it matters most on AI builds where the engine is reused across clients.*

---

## 8. Content and permissions

- The client confirms they own or are licensed to use every logo, photo, text and trademark they supply.
- The client indemnifies us against claims arising from content they supplied.
- We confirm the same for anything we create or licence for the project.

---

## 9. Warranty

- **30 days from launch:** defects in work we built are fixed free.
- Define **defect** — something we built not working as described in the proposal.
- Define what is **not** a defect: new features, changes of mind, breakage caused by the client editing things themselves, or a third-party platform changing its rules.
- No other warranty, express or implied — **wording here is the attorney's job.**

---

## 10. Care plan (where one applies)

- Monthly fee, what it covers, and what it explicitly does not.
- **Minimum term: 6 months** on Commerce and AI Sales Layer builds.
- Notice period to cancel after the minimum term: **30 days, in writing**.
- What happens on cancellation: hosting hands over, credentials transfer, we assist with migration.
- Our right to change the monthly fee, with [x] days' notice.
- Response times we're committing to, and the hours they apply in. **Only publish what you can actually keep.**

---

## 11. AI-specific clauses (bot builds only)

*This section has no standard template in this market. It matters — go through it line by line with the attorney.*

- The bot produces automated responses and **can be wrong**. Set out what the client is responsible for checking.
- Whether the bot may quote prices and confirm orders **on the client's behalf**, and what the client owes a customer if it quotes wrong.
- Which customer data flows through which third-party services, where it's processed, and who is responsible for it.
- What happens if a platform (WhatsApp, Meta, the AI provider) changes its rules, pricing or access mid-term.
- Escalation: which conversations must be handed to a human, and who decides.
- Who owns the conversation logs and the tuning work done on the client's bot.

---

## 12. Ending it early

- Either side may terminate with [x] days' written notice.
- If the client terminates: they pay for work completed to that date; the deposit is not refunded.
- If we terminate: we hand over completed work and refund anything paid for work not done.
- Credentials and domain transfer within [x] days **regardless of who ended it or why**.

---

## 13. Liability

- Cap our liability at the total fees paid under the agreement.
- Exclude indirect and consequential loss — lost profits, lost sales, lost data.

**Do not draft this yourself.** It is the clause that decides what happens on the worst day, and the exclusions have to be enforceable under Bahamian law.

---

## 14. Disputes

- Governing law: **The Commonwealth of The Bahamas**.
- Talk first, then mediation, then court — in that order.
- Which court, and where.

---

## 15. General

- Whole agreement clause (this document plus Schedule A is everything; earlier conversations don't count).
- Changes only in writing, signed by both.
- Confidentiality both ways.
- Signature blocks, printed names, positions and dates.

---

## Attach as schedules

- **Schedule A** — the signed proposal (scope, price, timeline)
- **Schedule B** — care plan terms, if applicable
- **Schedule C** — what the client must supply, with dates

---

### Before your first client, ask the attorney these

1. Does anything here fall foul of Bahamian consumer protection law?
2. Is VAT chargeable on these services, and at what threshold?
3. Is the liability cap enforceable as drafted?
4. Are there rules about handling customer data through overseas AI services?
5. Is a non-refundable deposit enforceable here?
6. Does the AI section need anything I haven't thought of?
