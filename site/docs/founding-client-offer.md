# Founding client offer

**Three seats. 50% off. In exchange for proof.**

---

## Why this offer exists (be honest about it)

A new agency in a small market has one problem: nobody has seen the work. THE PLUG 242 proves the system works — but it's *our* store, and a careful buyer will discount that. What closes the next twenty clients is three businesses that aren't ours, in three different trades, with real before-and-after numbers and a named owner willing to say it on camera.

That proof is worth more than the money we're discounting. Say this out loud to the client — it makes the offer make sense, and it makes you look like you know what you're doing instead of like you're desperate.

---

## The deal

**They get:**

- **50% off the build price** of any tier
- Full scope, same as any paying client — nothing stripped out
- Same 30-day fix warranty, same contract, same 4-hour reply time
- Their monthly care plan **locked at the founding rate for 24 months**, even when list prices rise
- First position in the case study section of the site

**We get:**

1. **Permission to document the work** — before-and-after numbers, screenshots, and the story published as a case study
2. **A video testimonial** — 2 to 3 minutes, filmed at their place of business, them talking
3. **A named quote with their business name and their face** — not "a satisfied client in Nassau"
4. **Access to the numbers** — response times, order counts, and after-hours captures before and after, pulled from the system
5. **A reference call** — they'll take a 10-minute call from a future prospect in the same trade

**Not negotiable:** the care plan is still required on Commerce and AI builds. Half price on the build is the discount; running a live system for free is not.

---

## The three seats — deliberately different trades

Three case studies in three industries beats three in one. A salon owner doesn't picture herself in a sneaker shop's story.

### Seat 01 — Food
*A restaurant, takeaway or food truck.*
**The leak:** orders come by WhatsApp all evening, the kitchen is busy, half the messages get answered late or not at all. Menu questions and "y'all still open?" repeat endlessly.
**What we'd build:** site with a live menu, plus the AI sales layer taking orders and handling the repeat questions.
**The number that will sell it:** orders captured during service hours when nobody could look at the phone.

### Seat 02 — Appointments
*A salon, barbershop, spa or clinic.*
**The leak:** booking happens in DMs. Double-bookings, no-shows, and the owner is doing scheduling admin between clients with product in her hands.
**What we'd build:** site with real booking, plus a bot that answers price and availability questions and books the slot.
**The number that will sell it:** hours a week off the phone, and the drop in no-shows once reminders go out automatically.

### Seat 03 — Parts and hardware
*Auto parts, hardware, building supply.*
**The leak:** "you have this in stock?" all day, every day, and each one needs somebody to physically go and look.
**What we'd build:** searchable catalogue on live inventory, plus a bot that answers stock and price questions straight from it.
**The number that will sell it:** stock questions answered with no staff time at all.

## How to pitch it

Say it plainly, in this order:

1. **"I'm taking on three founding clients at half price."**
2. **"I want yours because [specific reason about their business]."** — never "because you seemed nice." Because they're the busiest hardware store on that road, or because their DMs are visibly out of control.
3. **"Here's what I need back."** — the case study and the video. Say it before they ask what the catch is.
4. **"Here's why I need it."** — you're new, and you'd rather earn proof than buy ads.
5. **Then stop talking.**

**Do not** apologise for being new. It's the reason the price is half. Frame it as the trade it is.

---

## The line that closes it

> *"You get the same build everybody else pays full price for. I get to show people it works on a business that isn't mine. After these three, this offer is gone."*

---

## Rules for us

- **Three, and then it ends.** If you're still offering it at client eight, the price becomes the real price and every full-price client feels cheated.
- **Put the deliverables in the contract.** The case study and the video are contract terms, not favours. People forget favours.
- **Film the video within 30 days of launch**, while they're still excited. Wait three months and you're chasing somebody who's moved on.
- **Get the "before" numbers before you build anything.** Response times, message counts, missed orders. You cannot go back and collect them afterwards, and without them the case study has no left-hand column.
- **Pick businesses that are visibly busy.** A quiet business gets quiet results, and quiet results are a bad advertisement no matter how good the build was.
