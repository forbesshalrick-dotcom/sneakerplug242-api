# Discovery call script

**30 minutes. Your job is to talk for about 6 of them.**

The call has one purpose: find the thing that is costing them money and get them to say it out loud in their own words. Those words go straight into section 1 of the proposal. If you leave the call without a sentence you could quote back to them, the call failed — no matter how friendly it was.

**Write down their exact phrasing.** Not your tidied-up version. "I does be answering the same question from morning till night" is worth ten times more in a proposal than "the client reports high inquiry volume."

---

## Before you dial (5 minutes)

- Look at their Instagram and Facebook. How recent is the last post? Are there unanswered comments asking prices?
- Search their business name on Google. Do they come up? Is there a Business Profile? Is it right?
- If they have a site, open it on your phone. Time how long it takes to load.

*You'll use all three of these later in the call. Never lead with them — nobody wants to be told their business is a mess in the first minute.*

---

## 1. Open (2 min)

> "Thanks for making time. I'm going to ask a lot of questions and mostly listen. At the end I'll tell you straight whether I think I can help — and if I don't think so, I'll say that too."

*That last sentence changes the whole call. It tells them you're not just here to sell, so they stop defending.*

---

## 2. What they sell (3 min)

1. **"Tell me what you sell — like I've never heard of your business."**
2. "Who's your typical customer? Walk me through one."
3. "How many products or services are we talking about? Ten, a hundred, a thousand?"
4. "Does the price change — by size, by season, by who's asking?"

*Listening for: catalogue size and how complicated the pricing is. Those two decide the tier and where they land in the range.*

---

## 3. Where orders come from now (5 min) — **the important section**

5. **"Right now, today, how does somebody actually buy from you?"** *(Let them tell the whole thing. Don't interrupt. This is the money question.)*
6. "Where do most messages come in — WhatsApp, Instagram, calls, walk-in?"
7. "Roughly how many messages a day? On a busy day?"
8. "Who answers them? Just you, or is there somebody else?"
9. **"What time do they come in? Do you get messages at night?"**
10. "What happens to a message that comes in at 11pm on a Saturday?"

*Question 10 is the one that opens people up. Sit quietly after asking it.*

---

## 4. What they repeat (5 min) — **where the bot sells itself**

11. **"What question do you answer the most?"**
12. "What's the second one?"
13. "How many times a day, honestly?"
14. "When somebody asks if you have something in stock, what do you have to do to find out?"
15. "Does anybody ask the same things in another language? Creole, Spanish?"
16. "Do people send you voice notes?"

*Questions 14, 15 and 16 are add-on money — live inventory, multilingual, voice handling. Don't pitch them here. Note them and put them in the proposal.*

---

## 5. What it's costing (4 min)

17. **"If you could stop doing one thing tomorrow, what would it be?"**
18. "How much time do you reckon you spend on your phone answering people? A day, or a week?"
19. "Do you think you're losing orders? Where do they go?"
20. "Has a customer ever gone somewhere else because you took too long?"

*Question 20 usually gets a specific story. Write the story down. It goes in the proposal.*

---

## 6. Their history with this (3 min)

21. "Have you had a website before? What happened to it?"
22. "Did somebody build it for you? How did that go?"
23. **"Who has your domain right now?"** *(If they don't know — that's your opening for the ownership promise, and it lands hard.)*
24. "Is there anything you're worried about with this kind of thing?"

*Question 22 tells you what they've been burned by. Whatever it is — vanished, went slow, held to ransom — address it directly in the proposal.*

---

## 7. Practical (3 min)

25. "Do you have photos of your products? Good ones?"
26. "Do you have a logo, and do you have the actual file?"
27. "Is there a date this needs to be done by? Season, event, anything?"
28. **"Have you got a budget in mind?"** *(Ask it plainly. Then be quiet.)*
29. "Are you the one deciding, or is there somebody else in it?"

*Question 29 saves you from building a proposal for the person who can't say yes.*

---

## 8. Close (2 min)

> "Here's what I'm hearing: [say their problem back in their own words]. Does that sound right?"

*Wait for them to agree or correct you. If they correct you, you just saved the proposal.*

Then:

> "I'll send you a written proposal in the next three days. One fixed price, a date, and exactly what's included. No surprises after. If you like it, we start. If you don't, tell me — I won't chase you."

---

## Straight after the call — 10 minutes, do it now

While it's fresh:

- [ ] Their problem, **in their words**, written down verbatim
- [ ] Message volume per day
- [ ] The top two repeated questions
- [ ] After-hours pattern — do messages come in at night?
- [ ] Catalogue size and pricing complexity
- [ ] Budget signal
- [ ] Add-ons they revealed without being pitched (voice, language, stock, photos)
- [ ] Who owns the domain
- [ ] What they got burned by last time
- [ ] **Your call: which tier, and where in the range** — and why

---

## Things not to do on this call

- **Don't pitch the bot in the first ten minutes.** Let them describe the pain first. Then it's their idea.
- **Don't quote a price on the call.** "It's in the range on my pricing page — I'll give you the exact number in writing." Numbers said out loud get half-remembered and then argued about.
- **Don't promise Google rankings.** Not even casually, not even hedged.
- **Don't rubbish their old website or their old developer.** It makes you look small, and half the time the old developer is somebody's cousin.
- **Don't fill silences.** After a hard question, count to five. They'll fill it, and what they say in that gap is usually the most useful thing on the call.
